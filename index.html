Under the hood

From employer career pages to analysis-ready data

Employer-direct collection gives a different view from job boards, and conservative deduplication keeps repeated vacancies from inflating the counts.

Collect & preserve

Dedicated adapters for Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, WordPress Job Manager, Workable, Breezy HR, SmartRecruiters and Ashby, each handling its own pagination, detail endpoints and failure modes. Raw JSON/HTML is stored unchanged with timestamps and SHA-256 hashes, so cleaning logic can improve without recollecting history.

Standardise & extract

HTML stripped, locations and workplace type normalised, and missing values handled explicitly. A curated taxonomy extracts languages, frameworks, databases, cloud platforms and tools, keeping the matched text as evidence, and splits minimum from preferred requirements.

Classify, explainably

Rule-based classification by seniority, role, domain, employment and workplace type comes first. Optional scikit-learn (TF-IDF + logistic regression) and Gemini layers only suggest values for unresolved vacancies, write to separate fields and never overwrite the canonical data.

Publish & validate

jobs.parquet is the one-row-per-vacancy source of truth, with job_skills and job_requirements tables beside it. Dashboard marts are checked for job keys and joins, and three data-quality reports cover transformation, skills and the dashboard contract.

Applied statistics in R

Not just a p-value: effect sizes, assumptions and limits.

Four reports written from R Markdown, each stating hypotheses, test choice, exact statistics and an honest reading of what the sample can support.

Paired-samples t-test

Stress intervention

t(4) = 4.00 · p = .020

Mean stress fell from 7.4 to 4.2 (95% CI [0.81, 5.59]). With only five participants, I report it as promising, not generalisable.

Two-way factorial ANOVA

Memory: spacing × material

F(2,174) = 0.65 · p = .525

No significant main effects or interaction, and partial η² below .01. Visible line differences were negligible, not a reliable effect.

Chi-square tests

Categorical preference

χ²(1) = 4.74 · p = .030

Goodness-of-fit found no departure from 50/50; independence found a gender–preference link. Adjusted residuals (≈ ±2.38) showed which cells drove it.
